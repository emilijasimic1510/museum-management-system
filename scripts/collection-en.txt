The Last Tram|An empty stop and distant vehicle lights divide the frame into waiting and departure. The wet road retains a reflection after the light source disappears.
Window above the Market|An upstairs view reveals the geometry of market stalls as passers-by disrupt the grid. Window edges remain in the frame to mark the observer's presence.
Shore in the Mist|A thin jetty separates water from mist. A small figure at its end gives scale to a scene with almost no horizon.
Steps after Rain|Dry and wet surfaces alternate on worn steps. Reflected sky briefly covers the traces of long use.
Waiting Room at Noon|A window bar casts a shadow across empty chairs. An abandoned bag turns a quiet interior into a story of brief absence.
Rooftops from the North|Layers of metal and tiled roofs reveal different ages of one neighbourhood. Antennas and improvised repairs interrupt the sloping planes.
Bicycle by the Gate|The bicycle wheel repeats the arch of the metal gate. A tyre mark in the dust introduces movement into a still scene.
Centre of Gravity|Two rounded masses meet on a narrow surface. The apparent weight of the upper part contrasts with the calm balance of the whole.
Fold of the Shore|Layers of clay bend like a receding riverbank. Uneven edges retain finger pressure and differences in drying.
Bridge without a River|A slender arch rests on two unequal pillars. The void beneath becomes as significant as the metal enclosing it.
Stone Seed|A closed form has a shallow cut suggesting a place of germination. Differently polished surfaces change tone as the viewer walks around it.
Open Ring|A break in the circle allows the gaze to pass through. Its rough interior contrasts with the smooth outer surface.
Layered Wind|Thin elements stand at different angles. Their shadows suggest movement within a motionless structure.
Three Supports|Three supports of different heights carry a flat slab. Their uneven distribution invites close attention to balance.
Afternoon Wall|A cool window shadow cuts across a warm field of colour. Small irregularities in the brushwork suggest a wall absorbing daylight.
Blue Room at Six|The interior consists almost entirely of blue shades. A narrow orange door edge changes the depth and temperature of the space.
Fig Tree Shadow|Overlapping leaf shadows create a dense network of soft edges. Blank paper marks the brightest light.
Curtain Facing West|Fabric admits light in uneven stripes. Painted folds describe the object while dividing the surface into rhythmic fields.
White Table|Whites of different temperatures build the tabletop. A small bowl's shadow reveals depth almost concealed by the white paint.
Line of Dusk|A narrow band of light remains between a dark facade and the sky. Smudged pigment records the moment outlines begin to fade.
Reflection in the Corridor|A window reflection crosses from floor to wall. Perspective lines lead towards a light source outside the frame.
Neighbourhood Map|Streets appear as white passages between black blocks. One irregular route preserves the memory of a pedestrian shortcut.
Seven Passages|Seven openings repeat a basic shape with subtle changes in line pressure. The darkest passage contains the densest marks, rather than the most detail.
Letters from a Facade|Fragments of imagined shop signs form a new typographic composition. Overlapping colours reveal the printing sequence and process.
Rhythm of Balconies|Rectangular balcony fields form an irregular sequence. Deep cuts change the weight of black areas and the perceived distance of the facade.
Book of Short Walks|Each page records a short route through a neighbourhood. Blank pages mark pauses rather than unfinished work.
Rain Grid|Small dots gradually move from light to dark. Their density recalls a view through rain-covered glass.
Wheel Trace|A circular mark interrupts an even layer of printing ink. The impression preserves a unique meeting of pressure, paper and pigment.
Reading Lamp|An articulated support directs light without moving the heavy base. Visible screws show that individual parts can be replaced and repaired.
Folding Chair|A flat structure becomes a seat in one movement. Taut woven tape adapts to the body and reduces the need for additional material.
Workshop Radio|Large controls accommodate hands occupied with work. The casing has a replaceable protective grille and a handle that also guards the controls.
Teapot for Two|A short spout and broad handle support slow pouring. Matte glaze reveals variations in the vessel's wall thickness.
Box with Dividers|Movable dividers allow different arrangements of small objects. Visible joints reveal the method of hand construction.
Clock without Numbers|Two coloured circles replace conventional hands. Their overlap turns reading time into observing colour changes.
Weaving for a Small Flat|Dense and open areas of weaving vary the passage of light. The object forms a light partition without fully enclosing the space.
Reeds in January|Dry stems emerge from the cold surface of the water. A few bent lines indicate wind direction without depicting movement itself.
Pavement Herbarium|Plants grow in paving joints and beside walls. A low camera angle gives these small habitats the scale of landscapes.
Growth Rings|Concentric lines record uneven growth rather than perfect circles. A crack interrupts the rhythm and reveals tension within the wood.
Island after the Flood|Silt and branches form a temporary shore. A quiet palette leaves room to notice small traces of receding water.
Moss on Brick|Green patches follow cracks in old building material. The close-up obscures scale and brings the scene towards abstraction.
Atlas of Seeds|Seeds appear at a consistent scale without decorative frames. Shadows reveal the small structures that enable their dispersal.
Quiet Backwater|Almost motionless water repeats the outlines of the shore. A bright branch breaks the reflected symmetry and suggests passing time.
Portrait of a Reader|A book partly hides the face while the hands convey concentration. The quiet background keeps attention on the gesture.
Potter's Hands|Hands appear after work with traces of dried clay. Dark lines follow skin folds without idealising the surface.
Neighbour at Rest|The seated figure turns away from the viewer. The chair back and lowered shoulders convey the body's weight.
Self-Portrait through Glass|A reflected face overlaps the street beyond the window. Both spatial planes remain equally legible without a clear boundary.
Girl with Headphones|Closed eyes and a slight tilt of the head suggest listening. The headphone cable connects the face to the lower edge of the drawing.
Portrait of a Craftsperson|Only a few carefully chosen tools surround the figure. Paint on a sleeve says more about the work than the sitter's pose.
Conversation without Words|Two figures connect through their gaze and hand positions. The empty space between them becomes an active part of the conversation.
Room of Threads|Taut threads form a passage that changes with the viewer's position. Overlapping lines suggest denser walls without closing the space.
Archive of Touch|Casts of indentations sit beside pieces of worn fabric. Materials record repeated everyday touches without depicting the people who made them.
Mechanism of Silence|A lever's small movement passes through visible joints. Slow mechanics focus attention on anticipating the next movement.
Thresholds|Thresholds of different heights mark transitions between imagined rooms. Worn surfaces retain traces of the places from which they came.
Paper Cloud|Light elements hang at different heights. Their shadows form a second, darker layer on the floor and wall.
Shared Table|Objects by different artists surround an empty centre. The work explores how seating arrangements affect encounters and conversation.
Lines of Return|Curved paths return towards a shared point. Different tensions in the strips show that returning does not always mean taking the same route.
